# What do fiction writers, blocked writers and aspiring authors need?

Research for [issue #19](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/issues/19), using the method set in [issue #13](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/issues/13). Gathered 10 October 2026.

The question: what do fiction writers and novelists, writers who are blocked, and aspiring authors (the secondary group set in [issue #10](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/issues/10)) need from a game where they direct a story, it is recorded for them, and they are offered possibilities? Where their needs agree or clash with those of solo journaling RPG players (the primary group), that is flagged in its own section.

## How claims were verified

Direct page fetches failed in this session (the one tried, sciencedirect.com, did not resolve), as they did for the [earlier survey](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/blob/research/player-directed-story-games/docs/research/player-directed-story-games.md) and the [primary-group needs](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/blob/research/journaling-rpg-player-needs/docs/research/journaling-rpg-player-needs.md). **No source below was opened and read in full.** Each one was checked against web-search result snippets that quote or paraphrase the linked page. Read each citation as "the search snippet says this page says this", and open the page before quoting it anywhere that matters.

Sources are of four kinds: peer-reviewed studies of writers (writer's block research, and human-computer interaction studies of writers using AI), surveys by writers' organisations and a writing-software company, craft and prompt books by writers about their own practice, and writing-community blogs. Where a study was seen only through a secondary write-up, the citation says so.

Earlier map work was mined too:

- **Survey of player-directed story games** (branch `research/player-directed-story-games`). It is about games, not writers, so it is not counted towards any need here.
- **Current game write-up** (branch `task/current-game-as-built`). It describes the prototype, not what writers want, so it is not counted towards any need. It is used only in the last section.
- **Primary-group needs** (branch `research/journaling-rpg-player-needs`, `docs/research/journaling-rpg-player-needs.md`). Used only for the agree and clash section. Its needs are cited there as P1 to P14.

## Rules applied

- A need is phrased as something the writer wants, not a feature.
- A need has at least 2 independent sources. Sources with a shared origin count as one. In particular: the two Google Wordcraft papers count as one; Elbow and Goldberg both come out of the freewriting tradition and count as one; reports of Singer and Barrios's work count as one however many write-ups repeat it.
- Anything with one source is a lead, not a need.
- Every need is **from secondary evidence, not confirmed with players**.

## Needs

Each need below is labelled *from secondary evidence, not confirmed with players*.

1. **I want help getting past the blank page and started.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* In a survey of 146 writers about writer's block, the solutions they rated most useful included forcing themselves to keep writing and discussing ideas with others ([Ahmed and Güss, "An Analysis of Writer's Block: Causes and Solutions", *Creativity Research Journal* 2022](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/1069)). Gaiman says ideas are not the hard part; the hardest part is sitting down and writing, especially when there are no ideas ([Open Culture on Gaiman's "Where do you get your ideas?"](https://www.openculture.com/2013/12/where-do-great-ideas-come-from-neil-gaiman-explains.html)). In an experiment, giving writers story ideas from an LLM made their stories rated more creative, better written and more enjoyable, most of all for the least creative writers ([Doshi and Hauser, *Science Advances* 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)). Google's Wordcraft study reports model suggestions "serve to unblock writers" ([Yuan, Coenen, Reif and Ippolito, Wordcraft, IUI 2022](https://paperguide.ai/papers/b1901646-8a17-46aa-90c8-2a0641d2292a)).

2. **I want to draft without judging myself as I go.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Rose found blocked student writers were working from rigid rules and inflexible plans that got in the way of composing ([Rose, "Rigid Rules, Inflexible Plans, and the Stifling of Language", *College Composition and Communication* 1980](https://www.mikerosebooks.com/writer-s-block--the-cognitive-dimension.html)). Fear of criticism or rejection was the largest reported cause of block, about 29% of responses, per a summary of Ahmed and Güss ([Substack summary](https://suwca.substack.com/p/the-four-causes-of-writers-block); [paper](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/1069)). A survey of 874 writers found 95% feel self-doubt at least occasionally and 47% name perfectionism as a frequent challenge ([ProWritingAid, *The State of the Writer's Mind* 2026](https://a.storyblok.com/f/145420/x/9f967f96e4/the-state-of-the-writers-mind-2026.pdf)). Craft writers prescribe the same remedy: Elbow's freewriting separates generating from editing ([Oxford Academic, *Writing Without Teachers*](https://academic.oup.com/book/53321)), as do Goldberg's rules "keep your hand moving" and "don't cross out" ([excerpt of *Writing Down the Bones*](https://kayedacus.com/2014/05/05/natalie-goldbergs-rules-for-first-draft-writing-give-the-paper-the-colorful-coleslaw-of-your-consciousness/)); these two count as one. Lamott says all good writers write deliberately bad first drafts that no one will see ([Shmoop summary of *Bird by Bird*](https://www.shmoop.com/study-guides/bird-by-bird/part-1-chapter-3-summary.html)). NaNoWriMo participants valued the chance to silence the "inner editor" ([The Writing Barn](https://thewritingbarn.com/?p=15187)).

3. **I want a spark or a limit to push against, rather than total freedom.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* In two experiments, being required to use a given noun made rhymes more creative, and the effect carried over after the constraint was removed ([Haught-Tromp, "The Green Eggs and Ham Hypothesis", *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts* 2017](https://www.rider.edu/sites/default/files/docs/tromp_haught-tromp_PACA_2016.pdf)). Bradbury wrote lists of nouns ("THE LAKE", "THE NIGHT") and wrote from one until a character appeared and finished the story ([The Marginalian on *Zen in the Art of Writing*](https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/10/18/ray-bradbury-on-lists/); [Faena](https://www.faena.com/aleph/zen-and-the-art-of-creativity)). Kiteley's 200 exercises impose restrictions so writers can work "without having to wait for creative inspiration" ([Writer's Digest, *The 3 A.M. Epiphany*](https://writersdigest.com/wd-books/3am-epiphany)). Eno and Schmidt's *Oblique Strategies* cards exist "to help break creative deadlock" ([Soundfly](https://flypaper.soundfly.com/?p=14568)). The r/WritingPrompts FAQ says a good prompt gives "a little direction" but stays general: vague prompts may not start anything and very specific ones restrict ([r/WritingPrompts FAQ, via mirror](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/WritingPrompts/wiki/faq)).

4. **I want suggestions as raw material I can bend, not finished text I must accept or reject.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Writers using a multimodal suggestion tool did substantial work making "integrative leaps" to weave suggestions of varying relevance into their own stories ([Singh, Bernal, Savchenko and Glassman, *ACM TOCHI* 2022](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10366306-where-hide-stolen-elephant-leaps-creative-writing-multimodal-machine-intelligence)). Later work cites Clark et al. for the finding that a machine-written draft helps little because it diverges from where the author wanted to go, and little of it is kept ([Clark, Ross, Tan, Ji and Smith, IUI 2018, as cited in a later paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04007)). Thirteen published writers using Wordcraft saw most value in brainstorming, story details and world-building, and wanted a "brainstorming partner", not an author ([Ippolito et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030v1)).

5. **I want the story to stay mine: my choices, my voice, my authorship.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Storywriters who value the emotional side of turning ideas into words want to keep control rather than let AI lead; "if someone else or some agent is doing the writing... you can start to lose the sense that it's your own work" ([Biermann, Ma and Yoon, DIS 2022, via UBC](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/news/2022/06/dis-conference-paper-ubc-shows-what-writers-want-or-dont-want-ai)). Interviews with 20 creative writers found their values govern which help they accept ([Gero, Long and Chilton, CHI 2023](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145%2F3544548.3580782)). Professional writers found the model struggled to preserve style and authorial voice ([Ippolito et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030v1)). More than half of 15 theatre and film professionals felt they did not own scripts co-written with Dramatron ([Mirowski et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14958)).

6. **I want offered ideas to be fresh, not generic.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Professional writers found the model's output average or cliched ([Ippolito et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030v1); [Slashdot on The Verge's report](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/22/11/02/1653248/googles-new-prototype-ai-tool-does-the-writing-for-you)). Stories written with LLM ideas were more similar to each other than stories written without: individually better, collectively narrower ([Doshi and Hauser 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)).

7. **I want to find out what happens by writing it, not follow a fixed plan.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* King starts from a "what if" situation, not a plot, and watches the characters work their way out; he calls plotting incompatible with "the spontaneity of real creation" ([The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/stephen-king-situation/); [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=17434)). Bradbury wrote from a noun until a character appeared and finished the story for him ([SuperSummary on *Zen in the Art of Writing*](https://www.supersummary.com/zen-in-the-art-of-writing-ray-bradbury/summary/)). Goldberg's rule "lose control" says the same ([excerpt](https://kayedacus.com/2014/05/05/natalie-goldbergs-rules-for-first-draft-writing-give-the-paper-the-colorful-coleslaw-of-your-consciousness/)).

8. **I want to get through the middle and finish something.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* In a survey of 2,500+ US adults, 19% of would-be writers said they get stuck partway through a draft ([ProWritingAid consumer survey release](https://a.storyblok.com/f/145420/x/a7a70629fb/copy-of-prowritingaid-consumer-survey-release-final-docx.pdf); same company as need 2's survey but a separate sample). A writing coach describes writers abandoning projects in the "saggy middle" ([Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/writing-roadblocks)). NaNoWriMo's whole draw was finishing a 50,000-word draft; over 60% of registrants came back in later years ([Christian Science Monitor 2010](https://m.csmonitor.com/Books/chapter-and-verse/2010/1027/NaNoWriMo-Five-reasons-why-you-should-participate)).

9. **I want the story's facts kept straight so I do not contradict myself.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Novelists keep a "story bible" of characters, settings, world rules and timelines so they can check a fact without rereading the manuscript ([Jane Friedman](https://www.janefriedman.com/the-story-bible/); [series author on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/4388870-it-s-still-important-the-importance-of-a-series-bible-by-yolanda-sfetsos?tab=author)). Professional writers found Wordcraft lacked deep understanding of the story and its narrative consistency ([Ippolito et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030v1)). Users of an AI fiction tool list losing all prior context when a chat fills as a recurring problem ([Sudowrite feedback board](https://feedback.sudowrite.com/p/ai-consistency-and-workflow-limitations-overview)).

10. **I want the work broken into small pieces I can manage in the time I have.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Lamott's "short assignments": write only what you can see through a one-inch picture frame ([Baylor Graduate Writing Center](https://blogs.baylor.edu/gwc/?p=297)). NaNoWriMo turned 50,000 words into 1,667 a day, which writers found less daunting ([Southwest Writers](https://www.southwestwriters.com/are-you-ready-to-write-a-novel-in-november/)). Boice found blocked academics writing in brief scheduled daily sessions produced more writing and more ideas than those who waited for the mood ([Krashen's re-analysis](https://mail.sdkrashen.com/content/articles/optimal_levels.pdf)); note his studies used small groups and were never replicated ([UCSB critique](https://www.research.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/RD/docs/Write-every-day-a-mantra-dismantled.pdf)).

11. **I want someone to think ideas through with, who will not judge me.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Discussing ideas with others was among the most useful fixes for block ([Ahmed and Güss 2022](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/1069)). Creative writers weigh when to turn to a peer, a mentor or a computer for support ([Gero, Long and Chilton, CHI 2023](https://scrapbox.io/nishio/Social_Dynamics_of_AI_Support_in_Creative_Writing)). Wordcraft writers wanted the tool as a brainstorming partner and a beta reader ([Ippolito et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030v1)). NaNoWriMo's appeal included community, forums and write-ins ([The Writing Barn](https://thewritingbarn.com/?p=15187)).

12. **I want writing to stay enjoyable, not only productive.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Hobbyist storywriters are driven more by the emotional value of writing than by productivity ([Biermann, Ma and Yoon 2022, via UBC](https://languagesciences.ubc.ca/news-events/announcement-research-news-story/aug-3-2022-future-ai-writing-companions-changing)). Singer and Barrios found blocked writers took less joy in writing and daydreamed less ([Freelancers Union, drawing on Konnikova in *The New Yorker*](https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2016/07/15/scientists-discover-the-antidote-to-writers-block/); original study not seen). Participants in Clark et al.'s story-writing study found it fun ([University of Washington dissertation](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/bitstreams/cdbe25fb-364d-4a1f-bd43-593296961a21/download)).

13. **I want to trust that a tool will not take my work or pass its words off as mine.** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Of 1,700+ Authors Guild members, 90% want pay and 86% want credit when their work trains AI ([Authors Guild 2023](https://authorsguild.org/news/ai-survey-90-percent-of-writers-believe-authors-should-be-compensated-for-ai-training-use/)). 65% of fiction writers in a Society of Authors survey expected generative AI to cut their income ([European Writers' Council on the SoA survey 2024](https://europeanwriterscouncil.eu/soa-survey-uk-ai-2024/)). When NaNoWriMo declined to condemn AI in 2024, writers and board members left ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/nanowrimo-gets-backlash-after-defending-the-use-of-ai/)), and some authors blamed that stance as much as money for its closure in 2025 ([Self-Publishing School](https://self-publishingschool.com/nanowrimo-shutting-down/)).

14. **I want to practise and get better at the craft (aspiring writers especially).** *From secondary evidence, not confirmed with players.* Kiteley's exercises are framed as a hands-on workshop for exploring fiction craft ([Austin Kleon quoting the introduction](https://austinkleon.com/2006/01/20/mathematical-storytelling/); [Poets & Writers](https://www.pw.org/best-books/the_3_am_epiphany_uncommon_writing_exercises_that_transform_your_fiction)). Writers on a craft blog said prompts helped most while they were still learning how to build a story ([Kill Zone blog comments](https://killzoneblog.com/2015/10/writing-prompts-useful-or-waste-of-time.html)). A writing podcast argued prompts help beginners practise ([Writing Roots](https://writingroots.podbean.com/e/s21e1-%e2%80%93-writing-prompts-helpful-or-not/)).

## Leads

Single-source observations. Not needs.

1. **Directed mental imagery helped blocked writers** become more productive and confident (Singer and Barrios, seen only through [The Paris Review](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2016/03/15/destroy-writers-block-with-the-nuclear-option-and-other-news/)).
2. **Writers may value help with wording and review more than with ideas.** Thirty writers sought help in planning, translating and reviewing but found LLMs more helpful for the last two ([Chakrabarty et al., Creativity & Cognition 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12570v2)). This sits awkwardly with need 4 and is worth checking.
3. **Writers may not notice that suggestions improved their story** ([PsyPost on Doshi and Hauser](https://psypost.org/new-study-on-ai-assisted-creativity-reveals-an-interesting-social-dilemma)).
4. **Confident writers distrust AI on characters and dialogue** ([UBC on Biermann et al.](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/news/2022/06/dis-conference-paper-ubc-shows-what-writers-want-or-dont-want-ai)).
5. **Seeing which words are mine and which came from the AI** helped 13 creative writers keep a sense of control and ownership ([HaLLMark, CHI 2024](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/hallmark.pdf)).
6. **People claim authorship of AI-assisted creative text while feeling less ownership of it** ([Wasi, Islam and Islam, In2Writing 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00027v5)).
7. **Stepping away, or switching to another project, unblocks** ([Ahmed and Güss 2022](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/1069)).
8. **An offer of a different attitude, not an answer.** *Oblique Strategies* prompts the user to remember there are tangential ways to attack a problem ([Soundfly](https://flypaper.soundfly.com/?p=14568)).
9. **Questions as idea generators**: "What if...", "If only...", "I wonder..." ([Faena on Gaiman](https://www.faena.com/aleph/neil-gaiman-tells-us-where-to-get-ideas-from-for-writing)).
10. **Practising under a constraint keeps helping after it is lifted** (Haught-Tromp's carry-over effect, need 3's source; the only source for this specific effect).
11. **Disabled writers rejected the claim that they need AI to write** ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/nanowrimo-gets-backlash-after-defending-the-use-of-ai/)). Relevant to the accessibility floor set in issue #10.
12. **Professional and semi-professional writers experience block the same way** ([Ahmed and Güss 2022](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/1069)), which suggests the three sub-groups in this ticket can be treated as one for block-related needs.

## Agree and clash with the primary group

Primary-group needs (P1 to P14) are from [journaling-rpg-player-needs.md](https://github.com/jimmyNicholas/narinav/blob/research/journaling-rpg-player-needs/docs/research/journaling-rpg-player-needs.md).

### Agree

- **Something to start from.** Writer needs 1 and 3 match P2 (something to react to when I do not know what happens next). Both groups call it a prompt. Both also want the prompt pitched at the right openness: the r/WritingPrompts FAQ (need 3) and P4 (help that matches how much interpretation I enjoy) say the same thing.
- **Being the one who decides.** Writer needs 4 and 5 match P8 (I decide where the story goes). Both groups want offers that serve their direction, not replace it.
- **Surprise and freshness.** Writer needs 6 and 7 match P1 (surprise me even though I direct) and P3 (do not stall or repeat). Discovery writing (need 7) is close to journaling players' "play to find out".
- **Consistency.** Writer need 9 matches P7 (people, places and threads kept track of).
- **Reaching an end.** Writer need 8 matches P9 (reach an end rather than fizzle out).
- **Short sessions.** Writer need 10 matches P11 (fit into whatever time I have).
- **Wariness of AI.** Writer need 13 strengthens the primary group's lead 3 (some solo communities are hostile to AI). Across both groups, distrust of AI is now well sourced.

### Clash

- **Who writes the words of the record.** Writers want their own voice and authorship (need 5) and see AI prose as generic (need 6). Journaling players want to write as little as they like (P5) and not have record-keeping break the flow (P6), which favours Claude writing the record. Narinav's shape ("Claude documents what happens") serves the primary group and risks the secondary group feeling the **Artifact** is not theirs.
- **What the finished story is for.** Writers want to finish something (need 8) and to improve their craft (need 14): the output is a draft or practice. Journaling players want an ending that can be a loss (P10) and a record to keep and reread (P12): the output is a keepsake. The same **Artifact** may not serve both.
- **Complete record or chosen record.** Writers' story bibles aim at completeness (need 9). Journaling players also want consistency (P7) but want a say in which moments become the story (P13), and *Thousand Year Old Vampire* makes forgetting a feature. The two groups lean different ways on the same tension.
- **A writing aid or a game.** Need 14 (practise the craft) and need 2's focus on perfectionism pull Narinav towards a writing aid. Nothing in the primary list asks to be taught. Issue #10 already names this pull as an open question.
- **Plan or discover (inside the writer group).** Need 7 (discover by writing) and need 8 (get through the middle, where coaches blame a lack of plan) pull in opposite directions. The primary group shows no such split.

## Stopping point

Stopped on the rule in issue #13: the last three sources searched (Kiteley's *The 3 A.M. Epiphany*, Eno and Schmidt's *Oblique Strategies*, and Gaiman's essay on ideas) corroborated needs 1, 3 and 14 but added no new need. The session cap was not reached.

## Gaps

1. **Nothing was read in full** (see top). Highest priority to open and confirm: the Ahmed and Güss cause percentages (seen only in a blog summary), the Clark et al. 2018 findings (seen only as cited by later papers), and the Gero, Long and Chilton results beyond the abstract.
2. **Writer communities themselves were not read.** r/writing, the NaNoWriMo forums (now closed) and Absolute Write were seen only through blogs and aggregators. A browser pass would likely add needs and leads, especially from aspiring authors.
3. **Singer and Barrios** was seen only through popular write-ups (Konnikova via Freelancers Union, The Paris Review); the original study was not found.
4. **Boice's daily-writing findings are contested** and rest on small, unreplicated studies (need 10 stands without them).
5. **The ProWritingAid surveys come from a company that sells writing software** and now runs NaNoWriMo's successor; their method was not checked.
6. **No source on writers playing story games** (for example writers using AI Dungeon or solo RPGs as a writing tool) was searched. That is the overlap most relevant to Narinav and worth a follow-up.
7. **Every source is about adults.** Narinav is for children; none of these needs is shown to hold for them (see issue #14 for children's writing).

## How the needs bear on Narinav (observations, not decisions)

For later tickets. Terms follow `GLOSSARY.md`: Suggestion, Story bible, Artifact.

- Needs 1, 3 and 4 are what the three **Suggestions** a turn could do for writers: a spark to push against that the writer bends. The prototype offers three "beats" and then three Claude-written "renderings" of the chosen beat (current game write-up, "Turn loop"). The renderings are finished prose, which is what needs 4 to 6 say writers least want.
- Need 2 suggests the game should never grade or correct the player's own moves. The prototype refuses an edited sentence outside 12 to 20 words (current game write-up, "Turn loop"), a small rigid rule of the kind Rose found in blocked writers.
- Need 9 is what the **Story bible** is for, for both groups.
- Needs 5, 13 and the first clash above bear on the **Artifact**: whose words it holds, and whether a writer can take it away as their own.
- Need 14 and the fourth clash are a scope question for issue #20 (which needs Narinav serves), not something this note decides.
